One timeline

The arguments happened in sequence, and much of what was said only makes sense once you know who was answering whom. Disputed dates are marked as approximate.

  1. c. 1000–600 BCE
    The poems of the Book of Songs are composedPoets

    Three hundred and five poems from the early Zhou to the middle of the Spring and Autumn period. The line statements of the Changes take shape in the same centuries.

  2. 551–479 BCE
    ConfuciusConfucians

    In the same century the Buddha was teaching in India and the first Greek philosophers on the shores of the Aegean.

  3. c. 450–250 BCE
    The hundred schools contendOther masters

    Mozi, Mencius, Zhuangzi, Xunzi, Han Fei, the Art of War, Zou Yan's yin-yang and five phases. States were competing for survival, and any teacher with a workable method could find an audience.

  4. before c. 300 BCE
    The Laozi is already circulating in writingDaoists

    The bamboo-slip version found in a Chu tomb at Guodian, Hubei, in 1993 is the earliest known copy and holds about two fifths of the received text.

  5. 2nd–1st century BCE
    The core of the Huangdi Neijing takes shapePhysicians

    The medical classic applies the common ideas of its day, yin and yang, the five phases and the resonance of body and cosmos, to the human body. Walk back through that gate.

  6. c. 136 BCE
    Emperor Wu of Han establishes chairs for the Five ClassicsConfucians

    The Changes, Documents, Songs, Rites, and Spring and Autumn Annals become the state curriculum, the start of two thousand years of shared reading for the educated.

  7. c. 1st century CE
    Buddhism arrives in ChinaBuddhists and Chan

    The first translators borrowed Daoist vocabulary for Buddhist ideas.

  8. 3rd–4th century
    The "dark learning" of Wei and Jin; Wang Bi's commentariesDaoists

    Wang Bi died at twenty-three; his edition of the Laozi became the standard one. Tao Yuanming lived at the end of this age.

  9. 5th–7th century
    Kumarajiva translates the Diamond Sutra; Xuanzang the Heart SutraBuddhists and Chan

    Both translations remain the standard ones in the Chinese-speaking world.

  10. 638–713
    Huineng and the Platform SutraBuddhists and Chan

    From here Chan becomes the most Chinese form of Buddhism. Li Bai, Du Fu and Wang Wei were all born within decades of his death.

  11. 1130–1200
    Zhu XiConfucians

    He joined the Analects, Mencius, Great Learning and Doctrine of the Mean into the Four Books and rebuilt Confucianism in answer to Buddhist and Daoist questions. From 1313 the Four Books were the basis of the civil examinations.

  12. 1472–1529
    Wang Yangming and the ChuanxiluConfucians

    "The mind is principle", "the unity of knowing and acting", "extending innate moral knowing". His teaching later travelled widely in Japan and Korea.

  13. 1973
    The Mawangdui silk manuscripts are excavatedDaoists

    A tomb at Changsha closed in 168 BCE yields two copies of the Laozi, a copy of the Changes, and medical manuscripts older than the Neijing.

The classical texts are in the public domain; commentary and English renderings are our own.
