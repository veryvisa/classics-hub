Mind and the Way · A Map of Chinese Thought

Start from the body, and read how China thinks

People have been lying awake at three in the morning for a very long time. The first poem in the Book of Songs shows a man tossing and turning. The medical classic says his active energy cannot return to rest. Laozi tells him to stop trying so hard. Confucius asks whether he is fretting over gain and loss. And the Chan master Huineng says nothing out there is moving; your mind is.

These answers come from different books and different centuries. They borrow from one another and they disagree. This site puts them side by side under the same question, shows where they part ways, sets them against what researchers now know, and brings the result back to ordinary life.

Enter through the body

The most abstract words in Chinese thought (yin and yang, the five phases, the bond between body and cosmos, balance) all become visible in the medical classics as heat and cold, sleep and waking, the seasons, food. So we treat medicine as a gate. Each of the five ideas below leads to the door of a classic.
