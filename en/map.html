Reading the classics sideways

The question map: one question, many answers

The classics are usually read one book at a time. Here we turn them sideways: the question comes first, then each school's answer. Set next to one another, the answers turn out to be less a chorus than an argument.

How does a restless mind settle?

The phone is face down but the mind keeps scrolling; the body is in bed and the mind is already in tomorrow's meeting.

Daoists

Do not fight the thoughts. Put down what you are grasping and the water clears by itself.

致虛極，守靜篤。

Dao De Jing, ch. 16

Confucians

First know where you ought to come to rest. Once the direction is fixed the mind can settle, and only then think clearly.

知止而后有定，定而后能靜，靜而后能安。

The Great Learning

Buddhists and Chan

Nothing out there is moving; your mind is. Seeing that is already half of stopping.

不是風動，不是幡動，仁者心動。

Platform Sutra, ch. 1

Other masters

Xunzi does not talk about letting go; he talks about focus. The mind can hold many things, but while it does one, do not let another disturb it.

心何以知？曰：虛壹而靜。

Xunzi, "Dispelling Blindness"

Physicians

The medical classic treats a restless spirit as a bodily matter: with fewer cravings and less brooding, the body's vital energy can return.

恬淡虛無，真氣從之，精神內守，病安從來。

Suwen, ch. 1

Poets

Su Shi asked a woman who had followed her husband into years of southern exile whether life there had been hard. Her answer became a line for everyone far from home.

此心安處是吾鄉。

Su Shi, "Calming the Waves"

I know what is right. Why can't I do it?

Shelves of books, hundreds of saved articles, and the days look the same as before.

Daoists

Learning adds something every day; following the Way removes something every day. With less wanting, acting gets easier.

為學日益，為道日損。

Dao De Jing, ch. 48

Confucians

Wang Yangming's answer: if you cannot do it, you do not really know it yet. Knowing and acting are the beginning and the completion of one thing.

知是行之始，行是知之成。

Chuanxilu, part 1

Buddhists and Chan

There is no need to wait until you are ready. Confusion and awakening are one thought apart, and the next thought can be different.

前念迷即凡夫，後念悟即佛。

Platform Sutra, ch. 2

Other masters

Xunzi does not believe in sudden awakening. People are remade by daily study, the way a dye ends up deeper than the plant it came from.

青，取之於藍，而青於藍。

Xunzi, "An Exhortation to Learning"

Physicians

Zhang Zhongjing's method for learning medicine was plain: read the old texts honestly, then gather what works from everywhere.

勤求古訓，博采眾方。

Shanghan Lun, author's preface

Poets

The Songs praise a man by saying he has been cut, filed, carved and polished like bone and jade. Confucius's students borrowed the line to talk about self-cultivation.

如切如磋，如琢如磨。

Book of Songs, "Qi Yu"

How should people live together, and how should they be led?

Managing a team, raising children, living next to people from other cultures: should you step in more, or less?

Daoists

Stir as little as possible. The more you meddle, the more things fall apart.

治大國若烹小鮮。

Dao De Jing, ch. 60

Confucians

Lead by character so that people want to follow, like the pole star that stays put while the other stars turn around it.

為政以德，譬如北辰，居其所而眾星共之。

Analects 2.1

Buddhists and Chan

Practice does not require leaving family or work. Treating the people in front of you well is where practice happens.

若欲修行，在家亦得，不由在寺。

Platform Sutra, ch. 3

Other masters

The Mohists say care should not be graded by closeness and that mutual benefit is what lasts; the Legalists say do not count on virtue, just make the rules clear. Both disagree with the Confucians.

兼相愛，交相利。

Mozi, "Impartial Care II"

Physicians

The medical classic describes the body as a court: the heart is the ruler, the organs are officials with separate duties, and when the ruler is clouded everything below falls into disorder.

心者，君主之官也，神明出焉。

Suwen, ch. 8

Poets

When the autumn wind tore the thatch off Du Fu's own hut, what he thought of was everyone else without a roof.

安得廣廈千萬間，大庇天下寒士俱歡顏。

Du Fu, "My Thatched Hut Is Wrecked by the Autumn Wind"

What do you hold on to when everything changes?

A new country, a new career, a layoff, children leaving home: what used to be solid is gone.

Daoists

Things that reach an extreme turn back. Being in the weaker position is not necessarily bad; that is where the room to move is.

反者道之動，弱者道之用。

Dao De Jing, ch. 40

Confucians

The Changes gives the most direct answer: at a dead end you must change; change opens a way through; what gets through can last.

窮則變，變則通，通則久。

Book of Changes, Appended Statements II

Buddhists and Chan

Everything put together by conditions will not stay. Seeing this clearly is not pessimism; it is no longer being frightened by it.

一切有為法，如夢幻泡影，如露亦如電，應作如是觀。

Diamond Sutra

Other masters

The strategists take change as the normal condition: there is no fixed formation, just as water has no fixed shape.

兵無常勢，水無常形。

Sunzi, "Weak Points and Strong"

Physicians

Physicians look at change through the seasons first: all things sprout, grow, are gathered in and stored with the four seasons, and so do people; going against them brings trouble.

故陰陽四時者，萬物之終始也，死生之本也。

Suwen, ch. 2

Poets

Missing a brother he had not seen for seven years, Su Shi wrote at mid-autumn a line that refuses to console and consoles better than anything.

人有悲歡離合，月有陰晴圓缺，此事古難全。

Su Shi, "Prelude to Water Melody"

How do we face death and loss?

Parents growing old ten thousand kilometres away; and past midlife, starting to count the time that is left.

Daoists

Zhuangzi sees birth, toil, old age and death as four stretches of one process: what gave me a body, worked me, eased me in old age and rests me in death is the same thing.

夫大塊載我以形，勞我以生，佚我以老，息我以死。

Zhuangzi, "The Great Ancestral Teacher"

Confucians

Confucius declines to talk about what comes after death. He turns the question around: you have not yet understood living.

未知生，焉知死？

Analects 11.12

Buddhists and Chan

The Buddhists say the fixed self you are afraid of losing was never there to be found.

是諸法空相，不生不滅，不垢不淨，不增不減。

Heart Sutra, Xuanzang's translation

Other masters

Xunzi did not believe in spirits, yet he took funerals very seriously: seeing someone off properly is how the living make both ends of a life decent.

生，人之始也；死，人之終也；終始俱善，人道畢矣。

Xunzi, "Discourse on Ritual"

Physicians

The physicians promise no immortality. They speak of one's "allotted years": living out the span you were given is the good ending.

而盡終其天年，度百歲乃去。

Suwen, ch. 1

Poets

Tao Yuanming staged a debate between his body, his shadow and his spirit about how to face death. The spirit has the last word.

縱浪大化中，不喜亦不懼。

Tao Yuanming, "Body, Shadow, Spirit"
